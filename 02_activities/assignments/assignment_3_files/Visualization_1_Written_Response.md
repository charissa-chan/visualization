# Visualization 1 Written Response

This visualization, titled "Deaths of Toronto Shelter Residents (2010-2025)" was created using the "Deaths of Shelter Residents" dataset on the City of Toronto's OpenData portal. The dataset provides information on the number of shelter resident deaths from 2007-2026, and data collection is ongoing. Deaths are counted by month, and broken down by gender.

### Software Used
To create this chart, I used Python with the Pandas and Matplotlib libraries. I used pandas dataframes to structure and organize the data and to prepare it for charting. Matplotlib enabled me to plot the visualizations.

### Intended Audience
I created this visualization with the general public in mind, although policy-makers and advocacy groups for the homeless would also benefit from seeing the data laid out in this way.

### Information and Message Conveyed
I was initially curious about the effects of covid lockdowns on the homeless, and had a suspicion that lockdown policies and the public health crisis would have an adverse effect on their outcomes. To highlight the sharp increase of homeless deaths during the pandemic, I charted the numbers starting from a decade before covid hit (2010).

### Aspects of Design Considered
My priority was clarity, readability, and objectivity, as well as in comparability. As such, I chose a grouped bar chart to enable audiences to easily see the gender makeup of shelter deaths for each year. I also chose a style with colors that were vivid enough to be engaging, and with clear contrast so that the distinction between the bars indicating "male" and "female" was visible at a glance. For the sake of readability, I also rotated the ticks for the x-asix to provide more visual space between each tick. The style I chose also includes a grid, which helps viewers easily gauge where a data point falls on the y-axis.

### Reproducibility
Using code-based visualization, especially with a popular language like Python ensures reproducibility. I have also clearly laid out each step of data processing and plotting. These elements make my visualization reproducible.

### Accessibility
The accessibility is primarily ensured by the clarity of the title, which is concise and descriptive. The axes are clearly marked. I have also run the image through a color blindness simulator to ensure that the colors used do not prevent audiences with color blindness from easily viewing the chart. The relatively muted colors will prevent overstimulation in neurodivergent individuals, but the relatively high contrast between colors for the bars will still ensure easily comparison.

### Individuals and Communities Impacted
My hope is that the homeless population can benefit from this chart. It can raise awareness to the plight that they face, particularly when public health crises occur. Besides policy makers and advocacy groups, shelter service providers could also be impacted. They may be called upon to review their protocols for safety or emergencies or staffing.

### Features Included or Excluded
I chose to exclude the earliest years of the data to avoid overcrowding the visualization. As it is, the image is alread quite large (horizontally), I wanted to ensure readability, especially since I'm also including a grouped bar chart. I also opted to exclude data about other gender identities since their counts in the data were too low to include in a way that enabled meaningful comparison. This data is also incomplete as the city only began recording this information in 2020, the year of the pandemic. Including this information could be misleading as it might suggest to some audiences that the pandemic caused an increase in deaths in this category, or that before this year, no deaths occurred in this category.

### Underwater Labor
I opted to use an already-available style for my visualization, so there is certainly the labor of the designer who created this design and made it available. According to the information provided by the city, the data collected relies on reporting, not just partner agencies, but by individuals who serve homeless communities.
