# Visualization 2 Written Response

This visualization, titled "Overseas Visitor Spending in Ontario (1990-2017)" was created using the "Overnight Spending by Overnight Ovserseas Visitors" dataset on the Government of Ontario's Open Data Catalogue. The dataset provides information on the spending of visitors to Ontario from 1990-2022, organized by country of origin.

Dataset Used: Spending by overnight overseas visitors
https://data.ontario.ca/dataset/overnight-visitor-spending-by-country/resource/d8d10af6-ef9e-4412-94a6-7f87333edac2

### Software Used
I used Microsoft Excel to plot this chart. The dataset was already in .xlsx format, and since I only have elementary understanding of Excel, I relied heavily on the built in tools and easily navigated user interface to process and select data, as well as to modify elements of the design.

### Intended Audience
The intended audience is the general public who might be interested in tourism trends in the province over time. Policy makers and tourism authorities who are interested in evaluating the composition of visitors to the province may also be interested. This chart can help them understand the economic contributions made by different international markets.

### Information and Message Conveyed
This visualization shows the long terms trends in spending within this Ontario by major international visitor countries. It highlights the major effects of global recessions on tourism spending and although the European market has continued to lead in tourist spending within the province, the visualization reveals the increasing dominance of emerging markets like China and India. The visitor spending of China, in particular, has outpaced the UK and shows rapid increase.

### Aspects of Design Considered
For the sake of clarity, readability,and comparabiity, I focused on avoiding overcrowding the graph, and opted to select a limited number of major markets. This selection also focuses on comparabilty, as I have avoided selecting countries from only one area of the world, but have attempted to strike a balance between European and Asia-Pacific visitor markets.

### Reproducibility
The visualization was created using Microsoft Excel with an .xlsx dataset. It is highly reproducible since the same dataset can be downloaded, and excel tools can be used to filter and select data from different countries and years. The scope of my visualization has been clearly articulated by the ticks on the axes (1990–2017), and the selected countries have also been clearly identified by the legend. It is relatively easy to use Excel's standard charting tools to chart the data.

On the other hand, as I understand it, over the years, Microsoft has updated their in-built chart designs, and there is no guarantee that the current design chosen is an option that will continue to be offered in the coming years for anyone attempting to replicate the work I have done. 

Furthermore, the size of the chart and the font sizes of the titles and axes were manually adjusted based on what I thought looked clear. This information is less easy to replicate, and while the facts in the graph do not change, meaning that attempts to reproduce the graph will at least approximate my visualization, it may be difficult to achieve the exact dimensions and exact placements of the axes and title. 

Ultimately, however, I believe that the visualization is reproducible enough, given that the numbers are easily identifiable and selected in the dataset.

### Accessibility
I prioritized clear labels, readable font sizes, and a simple layout that reduces cognitive load. Limiting the number of countries included helps reduce visual clutter, making it easier for viewers to distinguish between different lines in the graph.

However, accessibility is largely dependent on Excel’s default options for visualization design. I have attempted to alter individual colors to increase contrast between lines and I have darkened the axes font color to improve their visibility as well, and overall, the design aims to be understandable for a general audience, even though the colors are not optimized for those suffering from color-based vision impediments.

### Individuals and Communities Impacted
This visualization is closely connected to the interests of the tourism industry, hospitality industries and local small businesses, whose profit is greatly impacted by traveler spending.

This chart might influence businesses and policy makers when make decisions about marketing their business and decisions about the province's infrastructure such as transit options which will need to meet the needs of visitors who come to Ontario. Furthermore, the tourism industry might choose to target their advertisements towards countries whose tourists spending is trending upward.

### Features Included or Excluded
The dataset notes that methodology for calculating the data changed in 2018, and that the covid outbreak (2020-2022) further constituted a disruption to tourism that was not representattive of actual trends. As such, numbers from 2020-2022 were excluded. Since the numbers from 2018 onward are not comparable to the numbers from years before, and that the years of the covid outbreak included no measured data, I have opted to exclude the years after 2018 from this visualization. This decision was made to improve comparability and to ensure an accurate representation of trends on tourist spending. I believe that the numbers from 2018 onwards are still important. However, given the changes in methodology, they warrant their own visualization, separate from this one.

I would have liked to account for the rate of inflation as a touchpoint for understanding the 'true' increase of spending among visitors to Ontario. Adjusting/Accounting for inflation would have helped provide a more accurate understanding of how much spending truly increased.

Furthermore, this information was not included in the dataset, but I imagine that it could be easily sourced by consulting other datasets. I would have liked to include a breakdown of which cities were most popular among these visitors, instead of simply thinking about Ontario as a whole.
